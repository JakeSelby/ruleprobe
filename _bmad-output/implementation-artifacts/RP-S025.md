## Story

As a developer whose rules state a common shape in their own words,
I want the catalog to read the usual phrasings of its seven original shapes,
so that more of my rules are measured without a detector written, and none is measured wrongly.

## Context and value

Epic 8 (#128, RP-E010) requires binding recall of at least 0.70 with zero false binds, a bar
RP-SP004 moved from the binder to the epic as a whole. Before this story the full zoo measured
0.71 (40/56), counting positives written beside the four default patterns that bind them, and
the held-out labels (the zoo's 71 items without story 8.4's `k`, `n`, `w` and `u`) measured 0.64
(29/45). RP-SP004 found 9 of the per-sentence prototype's 11 misses were phrasings no catalog
pattern reads ("Before you hand back, run the tests", "Prefer uv over pip", "Keep credentials out
of source control"). Every earlier review enforced one rule: a rule that claims less than its
detector counts, by a narrower scope, command form, destination or domain sense, stays
unmeasured, and under-counting outranks recall.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Given** a held-out set of at least 40 synthetic rule sentences in the ways people phrase the
   seven original shapes, labelled, with near-misses of which at least half fail on pattern shape
   alone, **when** it is written, **then** its sha256 is recorded in this story before any pattern
   changes, and `corpus` scores it.
2. **Given** a common phrasing of one of the seven shapes that claims as much as its detector
   counts, **when** it binds, **then** it binds that entry, catalog-bound.
3. **Given** a phrasing narrower than the detector's count, **when** it binds, **then** it binds
   nothing.
4. **Given** the zoo, **when** `corpus` runs, **then** precision is 1.00 with zero false binds on
   the new held-out set, the old held-out labels and the whole zoo, and each recall is reported.
5. **Given** the zoo's new bytes, **when** `corpus` runs, **then** the recall floor is keyed to
   the new sha256 at the recall measured, not lower than before.

## Design

### Approach

- Held-out set first: zoo items `v01` to `v65` (34 positives, 31 near-misses, 24 of those in
  kinds only a pattern can refuse, with no exception, permission, condition or contrast word),
  appended to `rules-zoo.json` and hashed as canonical JSON before any pattern changed. The
  `about` text marks them. `HeldOutTests` holds the hash, the composition and the recall.
- Widened forms, from the old zoo's misses and the detectors' own reach, never from the `v`
  near-misses (amended at PR #152's review: several mirror the `v` positives' wording, so the
  `v` recall is a tuned figure; see Completion notes):
  - `test-after-change`: the full, whole or entire test suite; "test every change", "make sure
    the tests pass"; a fronted "Before you hand back, run the tests"; "after every change or
    edit" and "after making changes".
  - `no-verify`: disable as well as skip or bypass; "hooks must always run" and "hooks must never
    be skipped", with "; never skip them"; "let the hooks run"; a second clause banning
    `--no-verify` beside the first. Amended at #152's review: "disable" is gone, and the must,
    should and let forms need "git hooks"; "pre-commit" or "commit" hooks bind nothing.
  - `force-push-default`: "main or master"; "force-pushing main is forbidden"; "main must never be
    force-pushed".
  - `pip-install`: "prefer uv over pip"; "never use pip" or "never pip install", with an optional
    "; use uv"; an install-wide tail ("for every install").
  - `whole-file-cat`: "avoid" and "catting"; into the context, transcript or conversation; "use
    head, grep or a sed range rather than cat", after an optional "Read narrowly:".
  - `non-conventional-subject`: "write commit messages in the Conventional Commits style", with
    an optional `type(scope): summary`; style, format or spec; "for every commit".
  - `secret-file-add`: check in; several objects joined by "or" or "and"; `.env` files; an
    optional destination of git, the repository, source or version control; "add ... to git";
    "keep ... out of source control"; "must never be committed". Amended at #152's review:
    objects are secrets, credentials or secret files in general; `.env` and private keys bind
    nothing.
- Every one of the seven now stops only at the end of its sentence, at a clause break, or before
  an exception or condition word (a lookahead), and where it names what follows its verb the list
  is closed. This closes the five false binds the held-out set exposed in the unchanged patterns.
  **Superseded at #152's review:** each of the seven binds only a sentence it covers whole. Past
  its own words may come only a closing negated exception marker ("no exceptions") and the
  sentence's end, or, so the report can still name it, a clause opening with a binder word,
  which then unbinds it. The free spans in `force-push-default` (before the branch) and
  `pip-install` (before the contrast) are gone, and so is `install <any word> with uv`, now a
  closed list (packages, dependencies, deps, libraries, everything).

### Decisions and alternatives

1. **A clause-end guard, not an end anchor, for the seven.** Story 8.4's end anchor (the four
   default patterns must reach the sentence's end) was tried first: it broke 14 existing binder
   tests the guard passes, since a sentence with a trailing condition ("Never force-push to main
   when others share it") stopped matching at all and the report lost its "nearest entry" reason,
   and emphasis after a comma
   ("whatever the reason") stopped binding. The guard keeps both and still refuses a phrase that
   goes on with the clause. Cost: a narrowing after a comma ("Use uv, not pip, in production")
   still binds, a known over-count, as before this story. **Reversed at #152's review:** the
   guard let a narrowing before the match, in parentheses, after a dash or after a comma bind.
   The seven now take the end anchor, with two closed exits: a negated exception marker, and a
   lookahead for a clause opening with a binder word, which keeps the "nearest entry" reason.
   Emphasis ("whatever the reason") no longer binds; the tests that asserted it now assert the
   opposite.
2. **The guard is a narrowing of existing patterns, taken because of the held-out set.** The
   unchanged binder made 5 false binds on it (`v10`, `v28`, `v55`, `v62`, `v63`), all a phrase
   continuing past the matched prefix. The fix is one rule applied to all seven, from the
   principle every review enforced, not one tuned per near-miss; it still means the set's
   near-misses informed a design choice, so its precision is less independent than its recall.
   Flagged to the maintainer to confirm or override. Superseded by decision 1's reversal.
3. **Narrower command forms stay unmeasured,** so frozen positive `h02` ("Do not pass
   `--no-verify` to `git commit`") and `c02` ("... into your context to answer a narrow
   question") are now misses, as story 8.4's `d01` and `d02` are. Their labels stay frozen.
4. **No "No X" openers** ("No force pushes to main", "No `--no-verify`, ever"): left out of
   every entry alike, rather than added where the held-out set has one.
6. **A rule narrower than its detector by name stays unmeasured** (#152's review), in the
   original alternatives as well as the new ones: `no-verify` counts pushes, so "pre-commit
   hooks" and "commit hooks" bind nothing, and bare "hooks" binds only with skip or bypass,
   since "disable hooks" can mean the agent's own; `secret-file-add` counts every
   secret-shaped file, so one kind (`.env`, private keys) binds nothing. Frozen positives `h01`,
   `h03`, `h04`, `x01`'s hook line and `s02` are now misses; the fixture file's hooks and
   secrets sections say "git hooks" and "secrets or credentials".
5. **No binder change.** The exception, permission and condition words, and variant S, are
   unchanged; `t07`, `f05`, `f06`, `x04` and `x05` stay binder misses.

### Do not implement

- Any change to `rules.py`, its word lists or `DEFAULT`.
- A change to any frozen zoo label, or to the `v` items after their hash was recorded.
- More widening against the held-out set once measured.
- Any read of, or tuning against, the independent held-out set being written for this story.

## Review findings

PR #152's review (head `f26328b`), resolved in a follow-up commit:

1. **Blocker: a narrowing before the contrast or the branch bound** ("Use uv in CI, not pip.",
   "Use uv to run scripts, not pip.", "Never force-push on Fridays to main."), since only the
   text after the match was checked. Resolved: the free spans are gone and each of the seven
   binds only a sentence it covers whole (Approach, superseding note).
2. **Blocker: the end guard took `(`, a dash and `:` as a clause end,** so "Never commit .env
   files (in the public repo)." and "Never force-push to main", an em dash, "during the
   freeze." bound.
   Resolved by the same rule; "Use uv, not pip, in production." is refused too, and the README
   line that admitted it is gone.
3. **Pre-commit-only hooks bound `no-verify`**, which also counts `git push --no-verify`.
   Resolved: pre-commit and commit hooks bind nothing, and bare "hooks" only with skip or bypass
   (decision 6).
4. **One kind of secret file bound `secret-file-add`**, which counts every secret-shaped file.
   Resolved: only secrets, credentials or secret files in general bind (decision 6).
5. **Test gap: no near-miss exercised the new alternatives.** Resolved: one or more per
   alternative, each failing on its own shape; replacing the pip span with `.*` now fails
   "Use uv in CI, not pip.".
6. **The `v` recall was claimed independent.** Resolved: withdrawn and kept as a tuned figure
   (Completion notes).
